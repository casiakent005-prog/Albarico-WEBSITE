A CULTURAL TAPESTRY OF FESTIVALS

Cebuanos celebrate their culture through festivals filled with color, faith, music, and community spirit. Across the province, these celebrations reflect the rich traditions, creativity, and identity of every town and city. More than just spectacles, Cebu's festivals are expressions of heritage and pride — offering visitors a meaningful glimpse into Cebuano life, warmth, and artistry. With more than 55 vibrant celebrations across the province, Cebu's festival scene showcases the passion, diversity, and festive spirit that make Cebu truly unique.

Sinulog Festival

Cebu City

One of the Philippines' most monumental celebrations, Sinulog honors the Santo Niño with a majestic blend of faith and artistry that unfolds over nine days. The festival begins with a solemn fluvial procession and culminates in a grand street parade where colorful costumes and rhythmic choreography animate the City. Sinulog is a magnet for both pilgrims and culture seekers, positioning Cebu as a spiritual and cultural hub of devotion and creativity.

JANUARY 15
Jan

Panuhug Festival

Santa Fe

A vibrant celebration of island heritage and craftsmanship, the Panuhug Festival honors the Santo Niño by showcasing Santa Fe's unique shell-craft artistry. The festival highlights panuhug—the traditional skill of threading seashells into intricate designs—with a lively display of faith, culture, and coastal livelihood. Through breathtaking shell-adorned costumes and rhythmic choreography, the event celebrates the island's marine abundance and the resilience of its people, positioning Santa Fe as a captivating hub of cultural pride and tropical devotion.

JANUARY 17
Jan

Kumbira Festival

Compostela

It features vibrant street dancing, colorful costumes, and a grand ritual showdown where different barangays compete. The town's dance contingent also represents Compostela in Cebu's massive province-wide festival, the Pasigarbo sa Sugbo.

JANUARY 4TH SUN
Jan

Sinulog sa Carmen

Carmen

A smaller yet equally spirited Sinulog, Carmen hosts its own version of the grand Cebu City festival. Devotees parade with Santo Niño icons, blending prayer and pageantry. It reflects faith's reach even in smaller Cebu towns.

JANUARY 20
Jan

Tuba Festival

Borbon

A high-spirited celebration of countryside culture and agricultural heritage, the Tuba Festival honors St. Sebastian by bringing Borbon's beloved local palm wine into the spotlight. Transitioning from its historical roots as the Silmugi Festival, the annual event highlights tuba—the traditional fermented coconut sap—as an enduring symbol of local livelihood, warmth, and hospitality.

JANUARY 3RD FRI
Jan

Panagbagat Festival

Poro

A deeply historical and unifying island celebration, the Panagbagat Festival (also traditionally known as the Tagbo Festival) honors the Santo Niño de Poro every third Friday of January. The festival commemorates the fabled unification of formerly feuding tribes on the island of Camotes.
